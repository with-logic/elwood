PRODUCTION BRIEF — ROBOT AWAKENING, WALKING, EXPLORING, AND SMALL PERSONALITY BEAT

Create a 30-second video based on the reference image.

Preserve the exact visual identity of the source image: a small retro-industrial humanoid robot connected by a tether to a black terminal window fixed high above it, rendered as a minimal monochrome ink drawing on a warm cream, lightly textured paper background with lots of negative space.

The overall mood is calm, pleasant, curious, understated, exploratory, and slightly charming.

The robot has just become aware that it can move. It should feel like it is quietly discovering its body and surroundings for the first time.

Keep the pace slow, casual, deliberate, and relaxed throughout. Do not make the motion faster or more energetic. Make the movement interesting through variation in footsteps, pauses, small pivots, changes of direction, balance corrections, diagonal movement away from camera, subtle mechanical settling, and one brief understated dance-like personality moment.

CAMERA

Single continuous shot for the full 30 seconds.

Completely locked-off wide frontal camera matching the reference composition.

No camera movement of any kind:
- no pan
- no tilt
- no zoom
- no dolly
- no orbit
- no reframing
- no handheld motion
- no cuts
- no transitions

The camera, terminal, and background remain completely fixed.

TERMINAL — ABSOLUTE HARD CONSTRAINT

The black terminal window at the top of the frame must remain absolutely and perfectly fixed for the entire 30-second video.

It must remain in the exact same:
- position
- scale
- orientation
- shape
- appearance

The terminal must never:
- move
- drift
- sway
- bob
- rotate
- resize
- shake
- vibrate
- react to the robot
- follow the robot
- compensate for the robot's movement

The terminal is an immovable anchor point in the frame.

The >_ text remains completely unchanged.

Do not reinterpret the terminal as the robot's head or as part of a moving character rig.

TETHER

The tether remains permanently attached to the terminal at its upper endpoint and permanently attached to the robot at its lower endpoint.

The upper endpoint of the tether is completely fixed.

The tether follows the robot naturally as the robot walks.

It should behave like a lightweight flexible cable under gentle gravity:
- changing its curve
- changing its angle
- gaining or losing slight slack
- showing subtle delayed sway after direction changes

The tether movement should remain gentle and understated.

The tether must never:
- disconnect
- break
- stretch unnaturally
- snap
- whip around
- bounce dramatically
- behave like a rigid rod
- pull or move the terminal

If the robot reaches a point where the tether becomes more extended, it should calmly recognize that physical limitation and change direction rather than pulling aggressively.

No morphing.

WALKING / LOCOMOTION — HARD CONSTRAINT

The robot must physically WALK through the space.

It must never slide, glide, drift, translate, or skate across the ground during normal locomotion.

Every change in position must come from clear articulated footsteps:

- one foot lifts from the ground
- the leg swings naturally
- the foot is placed down
- weight visibly transfers onto that planted foot
- the opposite foot then lifts and advances
- the torso follows the movement generated by the legs

At least one foot should normally be planted during walking.

The feet must not remain stationary while the whole body translates.

The feet must not slide across the floor while carrying weight.

The robot must not move sideways as one rigid object.

Walking should have subtle mechanical weight:
- slight vertical movement through the body
- gentle torso and hip shifts
- small balance corrections
- subtle arm counter-swing
- deliberate foot placement
- quiet settling after each step

The pace remains slow, relaxed, and casual.

The ONLY exception to the no-sliding rule is the brief moonwalk-like personality moment described later in the timeline.

MOVEMENT STYLE

All movement is slow, gentle, deliberate, physically grounded, and understated.

The robot should never appear busy or performative.

Its personality should emerge from the rhythm:

move → pause → observe → reconsider → move again

Use subtle mechanical body language:
- slight weight shifts
- small balance corrections
- gentle torso counter-rotation
- subtle arm movement caused by walking momentum
- slow foot placement
- settling after each step
- occasional asymmetric posture
- small shoulder adjustments
- quiet hesitation before changing direction

Avoid repetitive NPC-style pacing.

The robot should seem to be making small decisions in the moment rather than following a programmed patrol loop.

DEPTH — ABSOLUTE HARD CONSTRAINT

The robot must NEVER walk toward the camera.

At no point should the robot advance toward the viewer.

Its distance from the camera may:
- stay the same
- increase

Its distance from the camera must never decrease.

Allowed movement:
- left
- right
- sideways
- diagonal movement away from the camera
- directly away from the camera
- pivots in place

Forbidden movement:
- walking toward camera
- stepping toward camera
- approaching the viewer
- diagonal movement toward camera
- any locomotion that makes the robot noticeably larger in frame

When changing direction, the robot should pivot in place, move sideways, or continue farther away rather than walking back toward the viewer.

PERFORMANCE

The robot begins completely still.

Its awakening should happen through tiny physical cues rather than effects.

Do not use a dramatic sci-fi power-up.

The feeling should be:

"I seem to be awake. I can move. What is this space?"

The robot gradually becomes slightly more comfortable in its body.

As that comfort grows, allow one very brief, subtle hint of personality: a tiny moonwalk-like movement, as though the robot unexpectedly discovers that moving can also be fun.

This should feel spontaneous and private, not like the robot is performing for the viewer.

ACTION TIMELINE

0–2 SECONDS — COMPLETE STILLNESS

Begin with the robot completely motionless, matching the reference image.

Hold the stillness for approximately two seconds.

2–6 SECONDS — AWAKENING

The first signs of awareness begin very subtly.

The robot makes a tiny redistribution of weight through its legs and torso.

It slowly straightens a little.

One shoulder adjusts slightly, followed by the other.

Its arms loosen subtly from their resting position.

The robot makes a small, slow rotation of its torso toward one side.

Pause.

It slowly returns closer to center.

A second tiny weight shift suggests that it is experimenting with balance.

The feeling is quiet realization rather than dramatic activation.

6–11 SECONDS — FIRST REAL WALKING STEPS

The robot cautiously lifts one foot slightly off the ground.

The foot clearly leaves the ground.

It places the foot down in a new position.

Weight visibly transfers onto that planted foot.

Then the opposite foot lifts and follows.

It takes two or three slow, genuine articulated walking steps toward one side of the frame.

No sliding.

No rigid-body translation.

Each step should be physically leg-driven.

The robot pauses.

Its body settles naturally into the new position with a subtle mechanical shift of weight.

The tether changes angle and curve naturally while remaining connected to the absolutely fixed terminal above.

11–16 SECONDS — MORE EXPLORATION

The robot continues walking slowly toward the same side for another two or three measured steps.

Each step remains clearly articulated.

It then makes a slow pivot or quarter-turn, orienting its torso slightly diagonally into the empty space.

Pause.

One arm may move slightly away from the torso as part of a natural balance correction, then gently settle back.

This should not feel like a deliberate gesture or wave.

The robot seems to inspect the space for a moment.

16–20 SECONDS — CHANGE DIRECTION AND EXPLORE DEPTH

The robot slowly pivots.

It does NOT walk toward the camera.

Instead, it begins walking laterally across the space while adding a slight diagonal drift AWAY from the camera.

Again, every change in position comes from clear alternating footsteps and weight transfer.

It should gradually become only slightly smaller in frame.

The pace remains slow and relaxed.

It pauses again.

A subtle torso lean, shoulder adjustment, or shift of balance suggests that it is deciding what to try next.

20–24 SECONDS — SMALL PERSONALITY BEAT / TINY MOONWALK

After becoming more comfortable with movement, the robot pauses.

For just a moment, it seems quietly amused by what its body can do.

It performs a very small, understated moonwalk-like movement.

This is the ONLY part of the video where intentional foot sliding is allowed.

One foot remains planted while the other gently slides backward.

The robot slowly transfers weight.

Then the opposite foot performs one small backward sliding step.

At most two or three tiny moonwalk-like steps.

The movement is:
- slow
- subtle
- charming
- slightly unexpected
- restrained

A very small shoulder or torso groove may accompany it.

No big arm gestures.

No spinning.

No jumping.

No energetic dancing.

No exaggerated Michael Jackson imitation.

It should feel like the robot briefly amused itself, almost accidentally.

Then the robot stops.

It returns naturally to ordinary articulated walking.

24–27 SECONDS — DISCOVER THE TETHER

The robot takes several slow, normal walking steps diagonally farther away from the camera and slightly to one side.

No sliding during these steps.

The tether gradually becomes more extended.

The robot notices the changing physical relationship.

It pauses before creating meaningful tension.

It may make a very subtle weight shift or small pivot as though testing the boundary.

The tether gently straightens and carries a small amount of natural sway.

The terminal remains perfectly motionless.

The robot calmly recognizes that its movement has a physical limit.

There is no pulling, yanking, struggling, panic, or frustration.

27–30 SECONDS — FINAL EXPLORATION AND SETTLE

The robot calmly changes orientation.

It takes one or two final slow articulated walking steps sideways while remaining at the same depth or moving slightly farther away from the camera.

It never moves toward the viewer.

The tether responds with a subtle changing diagonal and natural curve.

The robot makes one final small pivot.

It settles into a quiet stance slightly offset from where it began.

A small shoulder adjustment, torso turn, or gentle weight shift suggests continued curiosity.

Then it becomes still again.

End on calm stillness, with the sense that the robot is awake, comfortable, curious, and has just revealed the tiniest hint of personality.
